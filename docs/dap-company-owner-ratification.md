# DAP Company Owner Ratification

## Status

**Ratified by:** Dipen — Founder, Owner, and Final Approver  
**Applies to:** DAP Company Operating Constitution and Guardian v3.0D organization design  
**Effective state:** Governance-approved; runtime implementation remains pending

This ratification resolves the remaining owner decisions recorded in the DAP Company Operating Constitution.

## 1. Trusted-User Access

Each trusted friend may access Guardian directly through an individual authenticated account.

Every user must have:

- a unique identity;
- scoped permissions;
- a private data boundary;
- an individual task and approval history;
- access only to authorized departments, tools, projects, and records.

Shared credentials are not permitted.

## 2. Trusted-User Approval Authority

A trusted user may approve actions that affect only that user's own:

- files and documents;
- email and calendar;
- applications and personal workflows;
- expense records;
- private projects and data;
- explicitly allocated resources.

Only Dipen may approve actions affecting:

- production or shared infrastructure;
- another user's information;
- company-wide policy;
- shared credentials or access controls;
- critical systems;
- permanent company structure;
- destructive or externally binding actions beyond the user's own scope.

## 3. Owner Visibility and Privacy

Dipen receives company-level audit visibility, including:

- user identity;
- time and type of request;
- department and role assigned;
- tools used;
- approval and policy decisions;
- task outcome;
- security or compliance findings;
- resource use and system impact.

Private user content is hidden from Dipen by default.

Private content may be disclosed only when:

- the user explicitly authorizes disclosure;
- a security incident requires investigation;
- company policy or law requires review;
- the content directly threatens the platform, another user, or critical data;
- the minimum necessary disclosure is approved and audited.

Audit metadata and private content must remain separate records.

## 4. External and Real-World Actions

External actions are approval-only in the initial operating model.

Before execution, DAP must present a preview showing:

- the exact proposed action;
- destination or affected account;
- material content or changes;
- expected impact;
- evidence and assumptions;
- rollback or correction options when possible;
- the identity of the approving user.

Explicit approval is required before:

- sending email or messages;
- modifying calendars;
- submitting job applications or forms;
- publishing content;
- making purchases;
- transferring money;
- modifying financial, identity, or external accounts;
- entering a legal, financial, or reputational commitment.

Approval must be scoped to the presented action. A previous approval cannot silently authorize materially different work.

## 5. Expense and Finance Data Strategy

The approved rollout is staged.

### Stage 1 — Initial

Use:

- manual expense entry;
- uploaded receipts;
- uploaded statements;
- user-provided budgets and goals;
- local offline analysis.

This is the default starting point because it is simple, private, inspectable, and compatible with an offline-first platform.

### Stage 2 — Later

Allow read-only extraction from authorized email receipts and uploaded document sources after per-user access controls and audit logging are implemented.

### Stage 3 — Separate security phase

Read-only financial-account integrations may be considered only after:

- dedicated credential isolation;
- secrets management;
- privacy review;
- threat modelling;
- revocation and incident procedures;
- explicit owner approval;
- explicit per-user consent.

DAP must not initiate transfers, trades, purchases, credit applications, or account changes without separate explicit approval for the exact action.

Financial outputs are decision support, not autonomous financial authority.

## 6. Temporary Staffing Authority

Guardian may create temporary agents and project teams without a fixed numerical limit, but only within a deterministic capacity policy.

The Chief of Staff and Infrastructure leadership must evaluate:

- available memory;
- CPU load and concurrency;
- model capacity;
- storage and database contention;
- task priority;
- expected duration;
- active-user fairness;
- critical-system safety margin;
- verification workload.

Temporary staffing must be reduced, queued, or rejected when capacity is insufficient.

Guardian must not create agents merely to simulate organizational size. Every temporary role requires:

- a defined objective;
- manager and department;
- allowed tools;
- resource budget;
- evidence requirements;
- expiry or project-completion condition.

Temporary teams dissolve automatically when their project ends unless a new approved assignment exists.

## 7. Permanent Hiring Authority

Guardian may:

- identify a permanent capability gap;
- design a proposed role;
- create and test a temporary version;
- measure performance;
- recommend title, department, seniority, tools, and authority;
- propose promotion from temporary to permanent status.

Dipen's explicit approval is required before any role becomes permanent company headcount.

Department heads, executives, independent control roles, and roles with privileged tools always require owner approval.

## 8. Governance Effect

These decisions supersede the unresolved questions in Section 11 of the DAP Company Operating Constitution.

The organization design is now sufficiently defined to begin role creation in this order:

1. Executive Office management services;
2. Product and Program Management;
3. independent Security and QA controls;
4. permanent department heads;
5. mapping current specialist agents into departments;
6. temporary-agent lifecycle and capacity policy;
7. multi-user identity and permission boundaries;
8. Guardian multi-task planning and execution.

No unrestricted broker activation is authorized by this ratification.
